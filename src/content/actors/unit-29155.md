## Overview

Unit 29155 is a Russian military intelligence actor with a public record that joins ordinary intrusion mechanics to unusually visible effects. The same profile contains scanning, stolen credentials, lateral movement and cloud exfiltration alongside defacement and destructive malware. Analysts should resist using the destructive history as a shortcut: an exposed service scan is not proof of a wipe operation, and shared tools are not actor-unique evidence.

## Attribution and cluster boundaries

The strongest identity statement comes from the September 2024 multinational advisory, which attributes the described activity to the GRU 161st Specialist Training Center, Unit 29155. The US Department of Justice separately named five military officers and a civilian in allegations concerning WhisperGate and related intrusions.

The 2025 Council statement adds a bounded Baltic case: Estonia's 2020 investigation connected Unit 29155 officers to unauthorized access at the Ministries of Economic Affairs and Communications and Social Affairs, with thousands of confidential documents stolen. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs was also targeted, but the source says no sensitive or non-public data was accessed there.

Ember Bear, Cadet Blizzard, UNC2589 and other labels provide useful search paths, but they originate from different analytic systems. The profile does not silently collapse all of their historical reporting. Saint Bear is specifically excluded as an alias because MITRE's current assessment treats it as a distinct activity cluster.

## Operational model

A defensible model separates the activity into four stages:

1. **Opportunity discovery:** internet scanning, subdomain discovery, public repositories, vulnerability scanners and exposed-device search.
2. **Access and expansion:** exploitation, password spraying, valid accounts, VPN access, PowerShell, tunnelling and lateral-movement frameworks.
3. **Collection:** local and email data, credential material and transfer to cloud storage.
4. **Effect:** public defacement, data publication and destructive disk operations when the mission requires visible impact.

Not every victim reaches stage four. The transition from broad access to a destructive or reputational mission must be demonstrated from victim-specific evidence.

## Recruitment and support structure

The UK's July 2026 profile reports that Unit 29155 officers tasked and funded Russian cybercriminals and OOO IMPULS, including recruitment of hackers and cyber specialists from Russian universities and academies. A coordinated EU action separately listed IMPULS and its owner for technical and material support to Unit 29155 cyber activity.

This is relevant organisational evidence, but not a shortcut for campaign attribution. A service, recruit or company relationship does not prove that every operation involving a related person or tool was directed by Unit 29155.

## Forensic anchors

- Correlate external scanning with subsequent authentication, web-server and VPN activity; scanner identity on its own is low-specificity evidence.
- Preserve web logs, reverse-proxy logs and appliance configuration before remediation. Edge systems can be both the entry point and the first destroyed evidence source.
- Review new scheduled tasks, PowerShell execution, remote-service use, archive creation and Rclone configuration in the same time window.
- Separate website modification evidence from internal destructive execution. The effects may be coordinated while leaving different artefacts.
- Search for exploit repositories downloaded by an operator, but do not report a vulnerability as exploited unless execution or victim evidence supports it.

## Analytic limitations

The public record is richest around WhisperGate and the 2024 joint advisory. It cannot reveal the full tasking history, every victim or every boundary between government and vendor clusters. The DOJ material contains allegations, while the 2025-2026 Council measures are administrative designations rather than criminal convictions. The current UK organisational description relies in part on public reporting. Tool reuse, targeting of Ukraine or a matching CVE does not independently establish Unit 29155 responsibility.
